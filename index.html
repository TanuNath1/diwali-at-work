01 Diwali cards

Light up a colleague's Diwali

Pick the badge that fits someone you work with. Add a few words and we'll send it to them as a Diwali card. It takes a minute, and they'll remember it longer than that.

Tips for a card they'll keep3 tips
  • Be specific. "Thanks for staying late on the launch" lands better than "thanks for everything".
  • Think beyond your team. The person in IT or finance who helped you out deserves one too.
  • Send it before the break. Diwali falls on Sunday, 8 November, so cards sent by Friday arrive while people are still at work.

02 Greeting emails

Send warm Diwali wishes

4 ready-to-send emails, copy in one click OpenClose

Four ready-to-send emails for different moments. Fill in the highlighted bits, make it sound like you, and send.

Before you hit send3 tips
  • Wish everyone. Not everyone celebrates Diwali, and that's fine. A warm note works for all.
  • Name a real moment. One specific win from the year makes the whole email feel personal.
  • Mind the calendar. Many colleagues in India take time off around Diwali. Send early in the week.
For the whole company

Subject: Wishing you a bright Diwali

Hi everyone,

Diwali, the festival of lights, is here. For many of our colleagues, it means diyas in the window, sweets on every table and long evenings with family.

It's also a good moment to look back at the year and notice the people who made it brighter. The colleague who picked up extra work without being asked. The one who always had an answer. The one who made Mondays a little easier.

This week, take a minute to thank one of them. A short note is enough.

Wishing you and your loved ones a happy Diwali, full of light, good food and rest.

Warmly,
[Your name]

03 Recognition campaign

Run five days of light

A 5-day plan and 4 shoutout templates OpenClose

Diwali celebrations stretch over five days. Borrow the idea for work: one small recognition moment a day, each tied to a Diwali tradition. Run it the week before the festival.

How to run it3 tips
  • Announce it 2 to 3 days before. Share the five themes in one message so people can plan.
  • Make it open to all. You don't need to celebrate Diwali to thank someone.
  • Use the badges. Ask people to tag each shoutout with one of the 8 badges above.
Day 1

Clean slate

Before Diwali, homes get a deep clean to welcome something new.

At work: thank the person who cleared a blocker, fixed a mess or tidied up a process nobody else wanted to touch.

Day 2

Rangoli

A rangoli is made one color at a time, often by many hands.

At work: recognize a cross-team effort and name everyone who added a color to it.

Day 3

Mithai

Sweets are shared with everyone, not only family.

At work: ask each person to send one sweet note to a colleague they don't usually work with.

Day 4

Diya

One diya can light another without losing its own flame.

At work: recognize a mentor or teammate who helped someone else learn and grow.

Day 5

Gifts and greetings

The festival ends with gifts, visits and warm wishes.

At work: managers share a roundup of the week's shoutouts, hand out badges and send the team off with Diwali wishes.

Shoutouts you can copy

Four templates for every direction. Swap in the details and post them on your recognition platform, in chat or by email.

Peer to peer
“

Happy Diwali, [Name]! Thank you for [specific thing they did]. You made [project or week] so much easier, and I don't think I said it enough. Sending a little light back your way. 🪔

Manager to team
“

Team, as we head into Diwali, I want to recognize how you [specific achievement]. Every one of you added something to it, like diyas lighting up a room. Thank you, and Happy Diwali.

Manager to employee
“

[Name], your work on [project] this year stood out. You [specific action], and it [result for the team or customer]. Thank you for bringing so much light to this team. Happy Diwali!

Employee to manager
“

[Name], thank you for [specific support or advice] this year. Your guidance made a real difference to me, and I wanted you to know. Wishing you a happy and restful Diwali.

04 Celebration ideas

Celebrate together, wherever you are

Office and remote ideas, plus a contest OpenClose

Ideas for the office and for teams spread across cities and time zones. Pick two or three, not all of them.

Keep it safe and easy3 tips
  • Use LED diyas indoors. Skip open flames and firecrackers at the office.
  • Keep it optional. Dress-up days and contests work best when nobody feels they have to join.
  • Plan around leave. Hold events a few days before Diwali, when most people are still around.

In the office

Rangoli contest

Teams design a rangoli at the entrance using colored powder, flowers or rice. Give them 45 minutes and judge on teamwork, not art skills.

Diya painting

Set out plain clay diyas, paints and glitter. People paint one to take home and one for the office.

Sweets potluck

Everyone brings a sweet from their home region or culture. Label each one with its name and story.

Festive dress day

Traditional or festive wear, open to everyone. Set up a decorated corner for team photos.

Light up the floor

String lights, marigold garlands and paper lanterns. Let each team decorate its own corner.

Hybrid and remote

Rangoli at home

People make a small rangoli with whatever they have, from spices to flower petals, and share a photo in one channel.

Festive background day

Everyone joins calls with a Diwali background or a real diya on their desk.

Sweets by delivery

Send a food delivery voucher so everyone can order their favorite mithai on the same day.

How I celebrate

A 30-minute call where a few colleagues share how their family marks Diwali. Great for global teams.

Team Diwali playlist

Everyone adds one festive song. Play it during the last meeting of the week.

Host a rangoli or decor contest

Works in the office, at home, or both.

  1. Pick 3 or 4 awardsChoose from the list, or make up your own.
  2. Collect entriesPhotos from the office floor, or uploads from people working remotely. Give everyone two days.
  3. Open a 24-hour voteOne vote per person per category. Keep it in a channel everyone can see.
  4. Announce winnersOn the last workday before Diwali, with a badge and a small gift.
05 Diwali gifting

Send something sweet

Gift ideas for office and remote teams OpenClose

Gifts are a big part of Diwali. Match the gift to where people work, and add a personal note so it feels like more than a box.

Gifting tips3 tips
  • Order early. Couriers get busy in Diwali week. Ship physical gifts at least 10 days ahead.
  • Check dietary needs. Offer sugar-free, vegan or nut-free options.
  • Go digital for remote teams. Vouchers and e-gift cards arrive on time, in any country.

Celebrating with a global team

Diwali is celebrated around the world, but not by everyone. A few things that help it feel welcoming to all.

1

Explain, don't assume

Share a line or two about what Diwali is. People enjoy learning about it.

2

Invite, don't require

Everything here should be opt-in. Thanking a colleague is open to everyone.

3

Plan around time off

Avoid big deadlines in Diwali week, and pick event times that work across time zones.

4

Know it's not one story

Sikhs mark Bandi Chhor Divas and Jains mark Mahavira's nirvana at the same time. Traditions vary by region and family.
